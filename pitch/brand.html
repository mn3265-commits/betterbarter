BetterBarter

Brand guide — the colours, the type and the voice, and the reason each one is what it is. Two colours, two faces, and one rule that governs both.

The idea

Pine acts. Goldenrod announces.

One colour was carrying the whole product, which made a busy board look like one long green sentence. The second colour is not decoration — it has a job, and it is a different job from the first. Pine is what you press: post, claim, confirm. Goldenrod is attention without urgency: a season starting, a feature announced but not open, a rating someone left you.

Deliberately not red. Nothing on this board is an emergency, and nothing here should feel like one.

Category colours

Six hues that survive colourblindness.

Categories are a fixed vocabulary, so they carry fixed colours — that is what lets someone scan a board instead of reading it. These six were run through a contrast-and-colourblindness validator, adjacent pair by adjacent pair, in the order they appear, and re-picked until every check passed.

Colour is never the only carrier: every category appears with its icon and its name beside it, so the board still works in greyscale, in print, and for anyone who cannot separate two of these hues.

Typography

A serif that sounds like a person, a sans you can read at 12px.

Keep the things a campus already owns in use.

Fraunces carries the warmth — its SOFT and WONK axes are what stop a serif reading like a plaque on a building. Plus Jakarta Sans carries everything a person has to read or click, so nothing important is set in a display face.

Display · Fraunces 600 · SOFT 70–100 · WONK 1 Body & UI · Plus Jakarta Sans 400/500/600/700 Never · the wordmark in all caps
Do

Set the wordmark as BetterBarter — mixed case, one word, no space.

Not

BETTERBARTER, Better Barter, betterbarter, or the serif at weight 800 — Fraunces has no 800, so the browser fakes it and the letterforms thicken into mud.

Do

Use the loop mark on its own where space is tight: a favicon, an app icon, a corner of a slide.

Not

Rotate, recolour or fill the mark. It is two arcs and two arrowheads in one colour, and it stays that way.

Voice

Say the number, name the assumption, stop talking.

The product's whole claim is that it does not overstate things. The writing has to match, or the claim is just a slogan.

Like this

“Both of you tap this. It ends the hold and adds +1 to each handoff count.”

“Estimated, at a displacement rate of 0.5 — the method is on the site.”

“No pilot users yet. Two verified accounts.”

Not this

“Revolutionise your campus sustainability journey.”

“Save the planet with every swap!”

“Trusted by students everywhere.”

Do

Separate what is measured from what is estimated, every single time a number appears.

Do

Name the thing a person recognises: a listing, a handoff, a campus. Not “transaction”, not “SKU”, not “user”.

Not

Exclamation marks, eco-clichés, or a claim we cannot show the arithmetic for.

Do

Let pine own every button, link and confirmed state.

Do

Let goldenrod mark what is coming: the season, the announced feature, the rating that arrived.

Not

Two brand colours in one control. If a button needs attention, it is already pine — make it bigger, not gold.

Not

Goldenrod as a warning or an error. Nothing here is an emergency; a refusal is written in words, in ink.
