BetterBarter

A dedicated higher-education campus marketplace that connects buyers and sellers to promote item reuse, avoid unnecessary new purchases, and reduce waste — a one-stop shop built on convenience, circularity, self-sustenance and safety.

Agung Nugroho & Tessa Wong · Columbia University betterbarter.vercel.app · live today
The Problem

Every year, predictably, the same thing happens to the same people.

Most incoming and outgoing campus residents in New York City want to buy, sell, give or receive used items — and go through fragmented channels to do it: WhatsApp group chats, Facebook Marketplace, eBay, physical thrift stores, Amazon Prime.

Poor Timing & Fit

Buyers may find a cheap item online, only to see pickup is out of reach. Sellers reach a general audience, with no way to find other campus members nearby.

No Trust or Verification

For most online transactions, community members agree to buy or sell to an unverified stranger, sometimes having to reveal their own personal details.

Waste, Waste, Waste

Buyers may end up paying more for what other community members are selling at a cheaper rate. Sellers donate or discard what they cannot sell on time.

Our Solution

Meet the BetterBarter Marketplace.

A one-stop shop based on the principles of convenience, circularity, self-sustenance and safety.

Campus-specific Community

Buy, sell, give, or receive items exclusively from verified campus community members. Filter by distance and choose to transact with people who live near you.

Environmentally Conscious

Two campus community members crossing a campus by foot or bike. No gas vehicle, no excessive packaging. Most importantly, items get a longer “life” and avoid being discarded too soon.

Built-in Safety

Chat with other members using only your first name. No phone numbers, no emails, no personal information exchanged. Meet up on or near campus, whatever is safe and convenient for you.

Variety & Relevance

Access new or used items from textbooks and furniture to fashion and baby toys, all from people nearby. We are more alike than we think.

How it works · built and running

Deployed on a live backend, not a mockup.

List it in two steps

Up to three photos, then a short form: category, condition, size, how you are listing it, and where you want to meet — from a list of real campus places, never a room.

Only your campus sees it

One verified university email per account, enforced in the database rather than the interface. Every school is an isolated Marketplace.

Claim, then meet

Claiming opens a thread and holds the item for three hours, so two people are not walking to the same lobby. Contact details are refused at the point of sending.

The community is the logistics

A fridge is not a one-person object. A listing can ask for two people or a trolley, and a student on the same campus takes that work for a fee paid directly between them — no van dispatched, so the transport term stays zero.

Both sides confirm, in person

Six digits, split three-and-three across the two phones. Neither app can read the other's half, so the count cannot be raised by one person alone.

It keeps itself tidy

An hourly job asks whether a listing is still there and retires it if nobody answers, so a Marketplace full of things that already went cannot happen.

What we will track

Realistic and simple: five numbers.

Circularity & self-sustenance

Items diverted from waste — used items given away or sold instead of thrown out or donated externally.

Number of items given for free.

Estimated CO₂e avoided per item, on the published model overleaf.

Safety

Percentage of active users with .edu verification — 100% by construction: the database will not create an account without one.

Reports per 1,000 transactions, and how many of them were actioned.

And one line on why the first number can be trusted. A handoff is not counted when somebody taps a button — it is counted when six digits, split three-and-three across the two phones, are put back together in person. Neither app can read the other’s half. A reuse figure one person can raise alone is not a figure, and every other campus reuse number we can find is self-reported or weighed at the back of a truck.

The impact model, v1.1 — published in full

Three levels, kept apart, so nobody takes our word for the third.

Measured
1 object

A confirmed handoff. Counted, not estimated — the only number here that is observed.

Estimated
3.45 kg

Mass kept in use, from a published factor table weighted to a mix of small things rather than one carried by bikes and laptops.

Estimated + adjusted
7.2 kg

CO₂e avoided, at a displacement rate of 0.5 — we assume only half of reuse events prevent a purchase.

The assumption most likely to be challenged, given away before anyone asks: a rented object earns no carbon credit at all in this model, because it comes back.

Sources: EPA Advancing Sustainable Materials Management (durable goods, textiles); EPA GHG Emission Factors Hub; EPA Waste Management Hierarchy; NCES IPEDS enrolment; WRAP, Environmental and Economic Benefits of Re-use. The factor table, the mix and the arithmetic are one file in an open repository.

The Business Model

Never on a physical item. Free or not.

Transaction Fee

A 1–2% transaction fee will be applied to higher-value service categories in later phases — tutoring, babysitting, car washing. No transaction fees will be applied to any physical item, free or not.

Institutional Partnerships & Licensing Fees

The main line. Priced against a cost the university already carries — move-out hauling, plus the circularity reporting it has to produce anyway — and it buys object-level, auditable reuse data no collection programme can produce.

Boosted Listings

A small, affordable flat fee for sellers who wish to boost their listing, especially if it is an urgent sale.

Advertisement Fees

Charged to local businesses — food and drink, clinical services, and other industries that do not cannibalise our own — who wish to reach a hyper-targeted, verified higher-education community.

Evidence of traction

What exists, and what does not.

What exists

A live product at betterbarter.vercel.app, open source. Row-level security on every table, an RLS-hardening pass after the security advisor flagged it, sign-up gated to academic domains server-side, rate limits in the database, a reporting and moderation queue, and a confirmation function that cannot double-count.

The impact model published in full — factor table, category mix, displacement rate, every source. All of it built and shipped by two people in five weeks.

What does not

No paying customers. No institutional partnership. No pilot users — two verified Columbia accounts exist and both of them are ours. No conversation yet with Columbia Housing, EcoReps or the Office of Sustainability.

We would rather state that plainly than dress it up. What we are asking to be judged on is a working product and a defensible method, not a user count we do not have.
