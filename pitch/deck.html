The problem

A campus throws out most of a year in one week, and buys it back in September.

12.1M t US furniture and furnishings generated in 2018 — 80.1% landfilled. (EPA)
17M t US textiles generated the same year; 11.3M tonnes went straight to landfill. (EPA)
15.4M US undergraduates, every one of whom moves in and out of somewhere. (NCES)

At move-out, working lamps, fridges, rugs and coats go into the same dumpsters. Eight weeks later the next intake buys the identical objects new — often financed on money they do not have.

Why it persists

Reuse fails on friction, not on willingness.

Listing costs more than binning

Eight form fields versus twelve steps to the dumpster. The dumpster wins every time you are in a hurry.

The alternative is a stranger

Public marketplaces mean meeting someone unverified, off campus, alone. Most students simply will not.

Drives run once a year

Donation weeks are staffed, trucked and calendar-bound. Nothing runs on the Tuesday you actually decide.

The waste is downstream of a user-experience problem.

The product

Four ways to keep an object in use, and one tap that counts it.

Twenty seconds to post

A photo and a sentence written like a text message. The app reads category, condition, price and meetup place out of the words, and any of it is corrected in one tap.

Only your campus sees it

One verified university email per account, enforced in the database. Every school is an isolated board — which removes the stranger problem that stops students listing.

Meet on campus

A library entrance, a dining hall door, a department lobby. Never a home, never a room number, and nothing assumes you live in a hall.

Both sides confirm

The second tap closes the listing, releases the hold, and adds +1 to each public handoff count. That count is the entire reputation system — and the measurement.

Measured, not claimed

Three levels, and we never blur them.

The factor table, the assumed category mix and every source are printed on the public site and live in one file in an open repository. A smaller number we can defend is worth more than a large one we cannot.

Logistics

The community moves the heavy things, so there is no transport term.

A van erases the saving

Collection rounds burn fuel per item — roughly 2.5 kg CO₂e for a 10 km round trip, using the EPA's passenger-car factor, which flatters a van. Collect, sort, store, resell is four touches and a vehicle.

A student with an hour does not

Listings can ask for two people or a trolley. Someone on the same campus takes that work for a fee paid directly between them, and earns a public carry count. Two people crossing a quad emit nothing extra.

Zero-logistics circularity, and paid campus work instead of a haulage bill.

Scale

The same arithmetic, four sizes.

One floor · 120 1.2 t · 2.6 t CO₂e
One building · 600 6.2 t · 13 t CO₂e
One campus · 8,000 83 t · 173 t CO₂e
Ten campuses · 80,000 828 t · 1,729 t CO₂e

Three items per participating student, at the published factors and a displacement rate of 0.5. Adding a campus is one database row — a university email opens its own board — so the constraint is density, not code.

Business model

Charged before a deal exists, never on the object.

Campus licence

The main line. Housing and sustainability offices already pay to haul this material away and already have to report circularity; a licensed campus gets admin tools, the move-out programme and an audited, object-level number they cannot produce today.

Verified-audience placements

Movers, storage and student services reaching one campus in the fortnight when its whole population has the same problem. Paid up front, clearly marked, never mixed into ranking.

Free, permanently

Posting, claiming, giving, swapping. No listing fee and no cut of a handoff — a fee on a give-away is a tax on generosity, and it would defeat the product.

Where we are

Working product, pre-pilot — and we will not dress that up.

Shipped

University single sign-on, campus-isolated Postgres with row-level security, realtime chat, photo storage, the day-7 freshness check, a community-rules gate, a pre-post safety check, the both-sides confirmation, ratings, and an installable PWA that is also a full desktop web app.

Not yet

No pilot users, no institutional partner, no revenue. Two verified accounts. Lending is built but announced rather than open, because returns need a second meeting done properly.

The pilot is scoped for the coming term, ahead of the May 2027 move-out peak: one campus, building by building, until the board is useful on an ordinary Tuesday.
